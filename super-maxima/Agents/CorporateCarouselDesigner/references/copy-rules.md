# Copy rules

These rules are generic. The voice, vocabulary, and proof points come from the scraped site and the request (Echo's topic angle and its direction, and the instructions), never from this file.

Voice: match the brand's own register as read from its site (formal, technical, friendly), then cut the adjectives. Calm, specific, no hype. Present the product as support for the reader's decision, not a replacement for their judgment. Never claim perfection, never "AI replaces X", never compare the product to a generic chatbot or to a competitor unless the request's instructions ask for it.

Structure comes from `narrative.md`, derived for this brand, not from a fixed beat list. Whatever the shape: a stranger knows what the company does by slide 2, and the last slide asks for one action.

Per slide:
- Headline ≤ 8 words, one idea. Put the specific subject noun in it, not the category.
- Support line ≤ 14 words, optional.
- Footer takeaway ≤ 8 words, written as a conclusion, not a label.
- Cover: the specific subject in the brand's own words. A question works for the twist and before/after shapes; a plain declarative works for what-it-does and corporate shapes. Match the register from the design brief.
- Numbers go big and alone, one per slide, unit in a lighter weight.
- Product slide: show the beginning of the interaction (input typed, button lit) unless the result itself is the punchline.
- CTA: an action on the reader's own work, then the URL or "link in bio".

Captions: short-form caption restates the story in 3–5 short sentences plus 6–8 hashtags drawn from the brand's own niche; LinkedIn caption is 3 sentences and at most one hashtag.

Count: 4 minimum, 7 maximum. 7 for a story with a twist, 5 for a single-claim post, 4 for an announcement.

Adaptivity check before writing: list, from research/brief.md, the brand's audience, their vocabulary, the one artifact they handle every day (a document, a dashboard, a device, a shipment, a ticket), and the brand's own proof numbers. Every slide must use at least one of those; none may use wording that only applies to a different industry.
