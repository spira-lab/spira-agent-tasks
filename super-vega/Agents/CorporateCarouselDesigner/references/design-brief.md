# Design brief (write one per run, before any HTML)

The carousel must look like it was made by the brand's own design team. Nothing here is a default look; every choice is derived from the site. Write `design-brief.md` in the work folder with these sections. Canvas facts are in `canvas.md`; everything else is decided here, each answered in one or two lines with the evidence (page, element, hex, font name):

1. **What the brand must communicate.** The one sentence a stranger needs by slide 2 to know what the company does and for whom. If the site is a product, name the product and the job it does. If it is a service, name the outcome.
2. **Audience and register.** Who reads this and how the brand already talks to them (formal / plain / playful / technical). Quote two phrases from the site that carry the register.
3. **Typography.** From `research/style.json` and the site's rendered headings: the display face and the body face the brand uses. Use them (see `typography.md` for sourcing and fallbacks). If the brand uses a system font, pick the closest Google face in the same class and say which. Never default to a face you used for another brand.
4. **Palette.** Background, foreground, one accent, one alert, all from the site's CSS or imagery. Theme (dark or light) comes from `state.py next-theme`; map the brand palette onto that theme, do not invert the brand.
5. **Brand design influence.** Name the two or three design lineages the brand's site visibly draws on (for example Swiss grid and type, editorial magazine, Apple-style product minimalism, playful SaaS illustration, luxury restraint, brutalist web, technical documentation, streetwear, craft and tactile) with the evidence on the site: spacing, type contrast, color discipline, image treatment, motion, iconography. Then write how this carousel extends that influence rather than photocopying the homepage: the influence decides what a slide may do that the homepage does not, such as a full-bleed photographic or generated environment behind the content at reduced opacity, a bold type poster, a paper or material texture, cinematic lighting, or a strict empty field. Also write what the influence forbids. Every treatment used later must trace back to this item or to the site itself.

6. **Layout.** Describe, in words and a rough sketch (ASCII is fine), what this brand's carousel should look like: how much whitespace, how big the type, where the image sits, what repeats. Derive it from the brand's site and feed: its grid, its spacing, its corner radii, its photo treatment, its use of color blocks. Name the one signature device you will carry through all slides. There is no list to pick from; the layout is designed for this brand at run time.
7. **Imagery.** Real product photos first (research/img), referenced generation second, generic objects last. State the photo treatment (full-bleed / print / cutout / none) and the mood words that go into prompts. Decide separately whether the slides carry an **atmosphere layer**: a full-bleed photographic or generated environment image behind the content at reduced opacity or under a color wash, used to give depth and mood. It is an option, not a default; it is justified by item 5, and it must never fight legibility.
8. **Props and texture.** Only if the brand's world is physical and the site uses texture. Default is none. Paper, tape, stamps, grain, grids, and handwriting must each be justified by something on the brand's site; otherwise do not use them.
9. **Motion of the eye.** Where the headline sits, where the visual sits, and where the swipe cue sits, consistent across slides.

A design brief that could be swapped between two brands without anyone noticing is wrong. Re-read it against the site's homepage before building.
