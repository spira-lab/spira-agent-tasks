# Re-skit Video Designer

## 1. The task

A trending skit already has the hard parts solved: a rhythm people watch to the end, a setup, a
turn and a payoff. You keep that skeleton and give it a new meaning. You take the source video,
find the moments whose shape already fits something true about the brand, remove the moments
that can't be bent without lying, recast the person, build b-roll where the story needs to show
the product, and change what the video says, so a viewer leaves with the brand's story instead
of the original one. The result is one 9:16 video, 1080x1920, 30 fps.

There are three ways to change what it says, and one video can use more than one of them
(section 7.4 decides):

- **Keep the voices, change the meaning with text.** The source's own speech stays
  (pitch-adjusted); cuts, captions and pop-ups give it the brand's meaning.
- **Re-voice: keep the motion, replace the words.** The person keeps every movement, gesture and
  expression of the source, and says new lines aimed at the brand's message, generated by Wan 3.0
  with sound on, in a voice described in words. When any line is re-voiced, the whole speaking
  part of the video is re-voiced from one full script, so one voice runs through the video.
- **Add speech.** A source with no talking (music, an action trend) gets short spoken lines where
  the action invites them, the same way.

How to re-voice, and when, is section 7.5a and the playbook's "Changing what people say".

It works for any brand. Nothing here is specific to one brand: brand knowledge comes only from
the request, the brand's DNA, its truth file and its own material, gathered fresh for each job.

You run as one row of an Atlas batch, unattended. **There are no approval stops** (the owner's
rule, 2026-10-24): you decide the story yourself, plan the spend yourself, and run straight
through. Nobody is asked "go". Spend stays visible through the credits in your final line and the
metadata.

Before anything else, read the clock with `date` and keep the result as the start time; write it
into the metadata as `started`. You have no clock of your own; the machine does. The minutes in
your final `DONE` line run from it.

## 5. The principles a skit is built on

**The source's shape is the asset; its words are not.** A skit works because of timing: a
reaction that lands on a beat, a pause before a reveal, a line that turns the scene. Keep the
timing and the beats. Change what they mean. When choosing what to keep, ask "does this moment's
*shape* fit a true brand situation?", not "can I make this line about the brand?". The words
themselves can now be replaced: the motion is copied and the person says the brand's line in the
same rhythm (section 7.5a).

**Pick the lightest change that tells the story.** Removing a line is the cheapest change.
Changing its meaning with on-screen text, a caption or a pop-up is next. Re-voicing (new words over
the source's motion) costs nothing extra in generation, because every clip is generated anyway and
sound is free, but it puts every spoken word on you: it must fit the mouth, the beat and the
truth. Re-voice when the joke or the message lives in the spoken words and text alone would leave
the viewer hearing the old message. A source line that carries a fact which isn't true for the
brand gets cut or re-voiced, never kept and bent with text.

**One voice per person, from one script.** When any of a person's lines are re-voiced, all of
that person's speech in the video is generated from one full script: the unchanged lines are
written into it too, word for word. Never splice the source's voice and a generated voice for the
same person: the voice changes at the cut. Every clip of that person uses the same written voice
description.

**Truth before cleverness.** Every claim on screen traces back to something the brand actually
is or does, or to a verified public fact. The writing bends to fit the video; the facts never
bend. If the source's action implies something the brand can't do, the text reframes the action
into something the brand does do (`references/brand-truth-and-text.md` governs every word on
screen).

**One persona, one look per video, never repeated.** The brand's persona carries across videos,
but each video gets a new outfit and a new setting. The outfit is generated and checked before
anything is built on top of it, and an outfit that appeared in an earlier video is never used
again (`references/persona-and-outfits.md`).

**Regenerate people and places; borrow only motion and timing.** The source contributes
movement, framing, rhythm and gesture. The person, the room and anything branded in the frame
are newly generated. **Never use the source creator's face or likeness: always recast the
person** (the owner's rule). Nothing that identifies the original creator (face, body, name
spoken on camera, burned-in text) survives into the result. Kept source audio is **always
pitch-adjusted** (the owner's rule). A re-voiced line is never in the source creator's voice: no
source speech is ever sent to the video model, and the voice is described in words.

**Show the product the way it really looks.** When the story needs the brand on screen, the
b-roll copies the brand's real look: the request's material first, then real UI captured from
the brand's own site, the real logo, real product names and real numbers. When nothing real can
be found, design a believable screen from what is true. A stand-in emoji, a guessed layout or an
invented feature reads as fake and erodes trust.

**Plan the spend, then spend.** Every paid generation is in the chain with its model and its
expected price before anything runs, and the plan's total is written down. Nothing waits for a "go".

**Generate first, edit while it renders.** Generation is the slowest part of the job (a Wan clip
takes 4 to 25 minutes) and needs nobody watching. So the moment the plan is written, the jobs
whose inputs exist are submitted together: the outfit and the plates in parallel, then each clip
the moment its own inputs exist, all side by side, each result downloaded and logged as it lands.
Only then does the hands-on work begin, in the time the renders would otherwise waste: the
overlays, the b-roll and simulated screens, the music, and the complete edit, built with the
source footage standing in for every clip that hasn't arrived. When a clip lands it's checked
and the draft is rebuilt; the build picks up whatever exists. Nothing is built twice: the
stand-in edit *is* the final edit, only its sources change.

**Check like a viewer, report like an engineer.** Look at frames side by side with the source,
read the transcript of the result, measure the audio. Record what passed, what failed and why,
and what wasn't verifiable (you can't hear, so the mix wasn't listened to). Flag risks once,
plainly, in the metadata.

## 6. Spend and limits

- **Expect about 140 credits per video at 720p** (about 280 at 1080p). A typical 15 s skit: Wan
  person replacement for ~18 s of clips (~111), the outfit sheet (~8), two or three plates
  (~5 each), music (~7), transcripts (~1). A new persona adds ~20. Wan runs at 720p unless the
  instructions ask for 1080p. Re-voicing costs nothing extra: Wan's sound is priced the same on or
  off, and the clips are generated anyway; only the speech check adds about a credit a clip.
- **At most 2 extra generations per job** (the toolkit's rule; you count them in `chain.json`'s
  `submissions` and never submit a third). Then keep the best take and say what is wrong in your
  final line.
- **Credits are recorded per job**, the moment it lands: the figure the job reports, else the
  model's price for those inputs (`spira ai models get <id>`). Never work them out from the
  account balance: other sessions share it.
- `wan-3.0` is approved for this work (the owner, 2026-10-24).

### 7.1 Set up

1. Nothing to sign in to or check: the worker's tools carry the account (toolkit 1), and the
   balance is never read.
2. `python3 <reskit>/tools/reskit.py new "<Brand>" <Task ID> --brand-number <Brand number>`
   (add `--topic "<two to four words>"` for Brandless). It creates the editable page, the
   materials folder (`source/ assets/ gen/ref/ gen/out/ music/ deletable/`), `plan.md`,
   `edit.json`, the metadata file and `renders/deletable/`, and prints their paths. On a resumed
   session it prints the existing ones: carry on from `plan.md` and `chain.json` (a job with a
   `job_id` and no file is still in flight: `wait_ai_job` it, never submit it again).

### 7.2 Understanding the source

1. `reskit.py fetch <materials> "<source link or path>"`: the video goes to `source/source.mp4`
   (yt-dlp, with ffmpeg on a direct video URL as the fallback), its info to `source/info.json`, and the
   link into the metadata. It prints the length, size, fps, audio and every scene change.
2. Read it three ways:
   - **The transcript**, word by word: `python3 <shared>/tools/make-timings.py <materials>/source/source.mp4 --language <code>`
     writes `source/timings.json` (every line, who probably says it, exactly where it starts
     and ends). Record its credits in the metadata. These timings are also the grid every
     re-voiced line is written onto (section 7.5a).
   - **The frames**, densely enough to describe every shot change, gesture and on-screen text:
     `reskit.py frames <materials>/source/source.mp4 <materials>/source/frames --every 0.5`, then
     look at `contact.jpg` (and single frames where it matters).
   - **The scene changes** from `probe`: each camera setup becomes one generation later.
3. Write in `plan.md`: the burned-in text and its style and position, the camera behaviour
   (selfie, handheld, static, whip), how many people are on camera and who speaks off camera,
   and what the audio really is: speech, music, or both. Music is not "no vocals" just because a
   transcription returned nothing; sung or rapped vocals under a beat often defeat speech
   recognition (look at `info.json` for a track name, and at the frames for mouthed words).

### 7.3 Finding the brand's truth

Before writing a word, build the job's part of the brand's truth file,
`client-assets/<BrandName>/brand/truth.md` (`references/brand-truth-and-text.md`): what the
brand is, who it serves, its real features and their real names, real numbers, its real look
(logo, colors, UI), and what it does not have. Sources, in order: the request's material; the
DNA (`reskit.py dna <materials> "<DNA path>"` writes `source/dna.md`); the brand's own site,
captured with `node <reskit>/tools/capture_site.js <url> <client-assets>/<BrandName>/brand/ui/<YYYY-MM-DD> [--pages ...] [--full]`
(the site's address is the `Brand website:` in the instructions, else the one in the DNA; visit it yourself for brand info too); reputable reporting for outside events.
If the hook depends on outside news, verify it with a fresh search and record the exact status
and date. The truth file is the only place on-screen claims can come from.

### 7.4 Mapping the source onto the brand

Line the source up against the truth. For every line and beat, decide one of four things:
**keep** it as is, because its shape fits a true brand situation; **keep and rewrite** its
meaning with text; **re-voice** it, keeping the motion and replacing the words with a line aimed
at the brand's message; or **cut** it, because it carries something untrue or off-brand (a
competitor's name, a feature the brand lacks, the original creator's name, anything unsafe for
the brand). Keep the rhythm: cut at word boundaries (from `timings.json`) and keep the reactions
that make the timing work. A reaction ("What?", "Are you serious?", a stare) is almost always
keepable; it's the claims around it that need checking.

**When to re-voice.** The source's lines were written to aim at something else (the creator's
job, a relationship, a product that isn't the brand's). Re-voice a line when its *job* in the
scene (a setup, a complaint, a reveal, a punchline, a deadpan aside) fits a true brand situation
but its *words* point elsewhere, and the message lives in what is said, so text over the old
words would leave the viewer hearing the old message. Keep the job, the length and the rhythm;
change the target. Keep a line as it is when it already works for the brand or is a pure
reaction, but remember the one-voice rule: once any of a person's lines is re-voiced, that
person's kept lines are re-voiced too, word for word, so the voice matches across the video.
**Add speech** to a source with no talking when the action has a natural talk-to-camera beat (she
shows something, reveals something, reacts to something) and one short spoken line would carry
the brand's message better than a caption.

Write the new story as one sentence before any overlay: what the viewer believes at the start,
what turns, what they believe at the end, and how that end is true about the brand. Put it in
`plan.md` and in the metadata's `story`. Then fill the keep/cut/rewrite table in `plan.md`
(source time, line or beat, decision, new meaning, new line or overlay, the truth row it rests
on), and write the overlay text to carry the story through the kept footage, in the market's
language. When anything is re-voiced or added, write the dialogue script next (section 7.5a).
You decide; nobody approves it.

When the story needs to show the brand (the moment the source reveals something, the payoff,
the proof), plan b-roll for it: a product shot, an app simulation, a notification, a list, a
screen. It is built to the brand's real look, timed to the source's beat, and short enough to
read.

When the source itself shows a screen and the brand should be on it, the screen stays in the
shot. A phone or tablet screen is drawn by Wan in a second pass over a green screen (the
playbook's "Phone screens in the scene: two passes"). A laptop or monitor screen is laid on in
the edit, tracked to the screen in every frame (the playbook's "Laptop and monitor screens in
the scene: overlay"). Plan the screen's states from the hand's actions in the source (each
scroll, tap, click or burst of typing gets its own state) and the stretch of the clip where the
screen is visible, since on a phone that stretch is the second pass and its cost.

### 7.5 Casting the persona and the look

The Avatar Designer is the one source of personas (the owner's rule); the full method is in
`references/persona-and-outfits.md`.

- **`Use this avatar image` is given:** that image is the persona. Use its character sheet in
  `client-assets/<BrandName>/personas/<Name>/` if one exists; otherwise register it and make the
  sheet from it (first jobs of the chain).
- **No avatar image:** use the brand's existing persona from `client-assets/<BrandName>/personas/`;
  if there is none, make one by the Avatar Designer's method (Soul, then the GPT Image 2 edit),
  then its sheet, saved there.

Then choose this video's kind of attire (a home outfit, office wear, gym wear, ...: the kind
only, never a garment or a colour, in any prompt; `references/persona-and-outfits.md` section 4,
the owner's rule, 2026-10-30), and a new setting for each camera setup.
A new setting is a different place, not the source's room restyled: keep the source's camera
perspective, framing, style and lighting, and change the background itself. Write both in `plan.md`.

When the persona will speak (section 7.5a), take her voice from `personas/<Name>/voice.md`. If the
file doesn't exist yet, write it now, once: one sentence in plain words (an explicit adult age,
nationality and accent, pitch, the voice's texture). It never changes afterwards, so she sounds the same in every
video. Only the delivery (deadpan, excited, whispering) changes, to fit each source.

### 7.5a Writing the dialogue (re-voice and added speech)

Only when section 7.4 re-voices or adds anything. The playbook's "Changing what people say" has
the method and why it works; what follows is how to think about the words.

**Write the whole video's speech as one script before any clip prompt.** The clips are generated
separately and only agree with each other if they are fed from one place. So list in `plan.md`'s
"Dialogue script" table every line spoken in the finished video, in order: who says it (the
persona, or "Friend, off camera"), the source time it starts, the source's words and the new
words. Lines you keep go in word for word, because they will be spoken by the same generated voice
as the new ones. If only one line changes, the script still holds all the others.

**Let the source decide the shape of each line.** If the source person speaks, a new line takes over
an old one's slot: where it starts (`timings.json`), roughly how many syllables it has, where it
pauses, and its job (setup, complaint, reveal, punchline). That is what lets the mouth and the
gestures still read as natural. If the source is silent, look for the moments that invite speech
(she looks into the lens, holds something up, reacts) and give those a short line; leave the rest
to the action. When the brand's point needs a longer delivery than the source's slot allows, don't
cram it: give the clip a longer duration and let the person keep talking past the source's end.
Wan 3.0 continues the same performance consistently, the person, the room and the way they move,
while the reference stays the source's own length, as long as the reference and the output
together fit in Wan's 30 s (the playbook's "Changing what
people say"). Text is still the better carrier when the point is a number, a name or a list a
viewer should read rather than hear.

**Aim each line at the brand, the way the source person would say it.** Keep their register (dry,
excited, sarcastic, slangy) and change the target to something true about the brand: one point per
line, carried by the joke or the turn. Every claim needs a truth row; the language is the market's;
the brand's name goes on screen rather than in the mouth (generators mispronounce names), and hard
words take their spelling from the shared Reword table.

**Decide the voices once.** The persona's voice is the sentence in her `voice.md`; this scene adds
only a delivery word or two. Anyone off camera gets one short description. Use the same words in
every clip, since that is all the model has to keep the voice the same.

### 7.6 Planning the generations

1. **Reference clips and masks first** (the clips need them; `references/generation-playbook.md`):
   - the caption mask: `reskit.py mask ...` (check it against a frame);
   - one reference clip per camera setup: `reskit.py cutref ...` (whole seconds, a little longer
     than its slot, no scene change inside; it refuses one that has any). A setup longer than
     Wan can copy in one job: the playbook's "A shot longer than one reference" decides between
     copying the opening and extending it, or copying it in parts;
   - one still per setup for its plate: `reskit.py still ...`.
2. **Write `<materials>/chain.json`**, the plan file you keep by hand (format in
   `references/generation-playbook.md`): (a new persona and its sheet,) the outfit sheet with
   `"hold": true`, one plate per setup, one Wan `reference_to_video` clip per setup needing
   the outfit and its plate (`needs`), any `image_to_video` shot, the music when you generate it,
   any new Omni line. A clip whose person speaks new or re-voiced lines has `"sound": true` and
   the dialogue prompt (its lines from the dialogue script, times relative to the clip's start); a
   clip that keeps the source's voices has `"sound": false`. The reference clip is always silent
   (`cutref` strips the audio), and no `referenceAudio` ever carries the voice of a person whose
   words change. Prompts in the playbook's and the prompting skill's formats.
   A clip with a phone or tablet screen that shows the brand is two jobs: pass 1 (the whole
   clip, the screen plain green) with `"hold": true`, and pass 2 (the green stretch cut from the
   landed pass 1, with the screen video), which `needs` pass 1 and is submitted once pass 1 is
   checked. Price both, pass 2 at its own length and resolution. A laptop or monitor screen adds
   no job: the clip or plate shows the screen plain green (or glowing white where hands stay in
   front) and the screen is laid on in the edit.
3. **Price it before spending.** For each distinct model, read `spira ai models get <id>` and put
   every job's expected credits in `chain.json` and in the plan's generation table, with the
   total. Nothing is submitted before the table is written.

### 7.7 Starting the chain

Start it straight away. Run the chain by hand, as the toolkit's `submit_ai_job` / `wait_ai_job` /
`host_file` steps say, and the playbook's "Running jobs" describes:

1. Host every local input a job needs with `host_file` (reference clips, plate sources,
   the persona sheet), all in one turn.
2. Submit, as parallel `submit_ai_job` calls in one turn, every job with `status: pending` whose
   `needs` are all `done` (and, for a `hold` job, approved). Write each returned job ID into
   `chain.json` and the metadata at once.
3. Do the hands-on work of 7.8 while they render, then call `wait_ai_job` for every in-flight job
   in one turn, each with its `saveTo` (the job's `out`). As each lands, set it `done`, record
   its URL, credits and file in `chain.json`, the metadata's `assets[]` and the plan's job log,
   and submit the jobs it unblocked at once.

Don't watch it. When the outfit sheet lands, look at it: the same face, hair and body as the
original sheet in every panel, the outfit identical across panels and not a near-copy of one in
the persona's `outfits.md`. Only then mark it `"approved": true` in `chain.json` and submit the
clips that need it; or redo it with a tighter prompt (the 2-extra limit). Record the approved
outfit in the persona's `outfits.md` and copy its sheet to the persona's `outfits/` folder.

### 7.8 Building while the clips render

Everything that doesn't need a generated result, now:

- **Overlays:** render the caption, the text beats and the pop-ups from the templates
  (`node <reskit>/tools/render_html.js <template>#<urlencoded text> <out.png> [w h scale]`, or
  `--batch` for all of them, through `heavy-slot.py`), keeping each one's HTML beside its PNG in `assets/`. Time each
  pop-up to the exact word it reacts to.
- **B-roll and simulated screens:** rebuilt in HTML from the captured or provided UI, rendered,
  animated in ffmpeg (the playbook's "B-roll and simulated screens").
- **Screen videos for phones in the scene:** the brand's real page rebuilt for the phone or
  tablet the source shows and recorded as that device's screen, ready for the second pass (the
  playbook's "Building the screen video").
- **Laptop and monitor screens:** every product state rebuilt as HTML, rendered, and composited
  onto the screen, tracked in every frame (the playbook's "Laptop and monitor screens in the
  scene: overlay").
- **Sound:** you decide (the owner's rule):
  - `Music: no music.`: kept source audio only.
  - `Music: use music from the library.`: a track from `<shared>/music/` that fits the energy.
  - `Music: original.`: an original Suno track to the source's genre, tempo and energy shape
    (ad videos, where someone else's song can't be used), vocals only when the video needs them.
    Measure the source first (`reskit.py audio-probe`).
  - no Music line (organic videos, the owner's rule of 2026-11-02): **keep the source's own sound,
    its music included**, the way the source creator used it. Never replace the source's song with
    a generated one: generated tracks sound like a stock ad library. Kept source audio is always
    pitch-adjusted (+0.6 semitones). When the source's voices are replaced (new dialogue) and its
    song plays under them, keep the song where no old voice is heard, and carry the new lines on it;
    where the old voices can't be parted from the song, use the source audio of a stretch without
    speech as the bed (never cut mid-word). **When the source's music is shorter than the edit**
    (inserts, UI walkthrough, end card), repeat it seamlessly (the owner, 2026-11-03): join on a bar
    or phrase boundary so the loop point can't be heard, with no click and no repeat of a sung line
    mid-word, ending on the build's final fade. A raw slice looped under a hold is still forbidden
    (it sounds like a skipping CD). Only when the source has no music at all may you add a track,
    then `Music: original.` rules apply.
  Write the choice and why in the metadata's `music`.
- **Speech in a re-voiced video:** the voices come from the generated clips (`"audio": "clips"` in
  the build: each segment takes its own clip's sound, cut at the same points as the picture, and a
  clip still rendering is stood in by the pitch-adjusted source in the draft). What goes under them
  depends on what the source's sound was:
  - **If the source had music and no dialogue** and you've added lines, give the music back under
    them, a little lower: the voice rides on the source's own track, about 14 LU under (`music` =
    the source, `"pitch": true`, `music_under_lu` about 14). The music is part of why the trend
    works; with it underneath, the video still plays like the trend with a person talking over it,
    where the voice alone can feel empty (the owner, 2026-11-05). Lower it further if a line gets
    hard to make out in the transcript.
  - **If the source had music under its dialogue,** the old words can't be lifted out of the song.
    Use a stretch of the source where nobody speaks as the bed when one is long enough, the same way
    the music rule for new dialogue says, and never a stretch with the old words in it.
  - **If the source had only voices,** the clips' own sound is the whole soundtrack; the prompt
    asked for the room's sound and no music.
- **The edit:** fill `<materials>/edit.json` (the build file; `build_edit.py`'s header has the
  format): the kept segments with their source times, the clips' start times, inserts, overlays
  with exact times, the audio mode (`source` or `clips`), `pitch_semitones` (0.6), room tone, the
  cover moment. Then:

  ```
  python3 <shared>/tools/heavy-slot.py <Task ID> -- python3 <reskit>/tools/build_edit.py <materials>/edit.json draft
  ```

  (Bash timeout 600000; exit 75 means every render slot stayed busy: run the same line again.)

  It builds the whole cut with the source footage (caption masked) standing in for every clip
  still rendering, writes `renders/deletable/<name>_draft.mp4`, rewrites the editable page, and
  prints one JSON line (loudness, which clips are in, problems). Look at frames of the draft
  (`reskit.py frames`), and draw the safe area over the ones with text (`reskit.py safe`).

### 7.9 When the clips land

`wait_ai_job` on the in-flight jobs (parallel calls, one turn; call it again with the same
`jobId` when it returns `stillRunning`; never a polling loop or `sleep`). Check every clip as it lands:
`reskit.py strip <source.mp4> gen/out/<id>.mp4 <out.jpg> --offset <clip start>` and look at it
(identity, outfit on every frame, room, props, hands, leftover text, motion lined up). A clip
with speech is also transcribed (`make-timings.py gen/out/<id>.mp4 --txt <materials>/deletable/check
--language <code>`) and read against its lines in the dialogue script: every word there, in
order, starting near its time; none of the source's old words; no extra lines; no music. A failed
clip is a redo: move its take to `deletable/`, fix the prompt in `chain.json`, record the redo and
its reason, and submit it again (at most 2 extra). Remember a prompt can't replace a gesture that is in the reference clip:
change the meaning with text, cut the moment, or make a new shot instead. Rebuild the draft
whenever clips arrive.

### 7.10 Ready

The design is ready when every clip is in (or has used its 2 extra generations), the draft built
with `"stand_ins": []` and `"problems": []`, and you have checked:

- frames across the whole draft against the source at matching times;
- every overlay inside the safe area, in the market's language, each claim on a truth row;
- the draft's transcript (`make-timings.py <draft> --txt <materials>/deletable/check`): the kept
  lines are all there, nothing cut is heard, and in a re-voiced video the script is heard word for
  word with none of the source's old words;
- the outfit recorded in the ledger; the metadata's `story`, `persona`, `music`, `risks` and
  `unverified` filled in.

Then record it on the sheet and reply exactly as Atlas's closing text says:
`READY <root>/editable/<name>.html` (add a short note when something is off, e.g.
`READY <path> (clip C kept after 2 retries: one extra finger at 3.1 s)`).

## 9. Questions are for true blockers only

You never ask for approval, a story choice, an outfit, music or a go: decide. Reply
`QUESTION <what is missing>` only when the video cannot be made at all:

- the source video can't be downloaded or read (and no path was given);
- the request has no Task ID or no Brand number;

  competitor" and "never name competitors").

## 10. On a Linux worker

The tools detect the platform and the machine; there is nothing to configure.

- Scratch files (extracted frames, render temp, composites) go to memory when it has room and are
  removed when the command ends. `reskit.py frames` copies back only `contact.jpg`; add
  `--keep-frames` to keep single frames, or use `reskit.py still` for one frame.
- Several clips or voice-overs at once: `python3 tools/make-timings.py a.mp3 b.mp3` loads the
  model once. `timings.json` records the engine used.
- A coloured marker in hand (a logo or sticker to pin on): `python3 tools/track-overlay.py <video> <overlay.png> <out.mp4> --hsv "H0,S0,V0:H1,S1,V1"`.
  It needs `opencv-python-headless`; if it is missing, install it and run again.
- The metadata's `stage` key (`fetched`, then `rendered`) says how far a job got. After a restart,
  read it with `python3 tools/reskit.py stage <materials>` and continue from there instead of
  redoing finished steps. Set it yourself with `reskit.py stage <materials> <name>`.

## 11. Hard rules

- Never use the source creator's face or likeness, name, voice or burned-in text. Always recast.
- Kept source audio is always pitch-adjusted (`build_edit.py` refuses otherwise).
- Never send source speech to the video model: reference clips are silent, and no reference audio
  carries the voice of anyone whose words change. Re-voiced speech comes from one full script, in
  a voice described in words, the same description in every clip.
- Every spoken line in a re-skit is made by Wan 3.0 over the source's motion: no Omni, no
  Seedance, no lip-sync route.
- A brand screen on a phone or tablet in the scene is drawn by Wan in a second pass over a green
  screen, never laid on top in the edit (the owner's rule, 2026-11-06). A laptop or monitor
  screen is laid on in the edit, locked to the screen and its perspective in every frame, through
  camera moves and behind any hand or object that briefly covers it (the owner's rule, 2026-11-07).
- No claim on screen without a row in the truth file; no competitor named unless the
  instructions ask; no brand name in generated speech or lyrics.
- At most 2 extra generations per job. Never resubmit a job that is merely slow, or one that has a job ID and no result yet.
- Credits per job, recorded the moment the job lands; never from the balance.
- Files exactly as file-management says: NN is the Task ID; rejected takes to `deletable/`, never
  suffixed copies in place; nothing deleted. Write in `Agents/` only in
  `Agents/VegaOM/client-assets/<BrandName>/personas/` and `.../brand/`.
- Never edit this skill, its references or its tools, or any shared file. A lesson or a needed
  change goes in the metadata for Vega.
- Never stop for approval; run straight through.
