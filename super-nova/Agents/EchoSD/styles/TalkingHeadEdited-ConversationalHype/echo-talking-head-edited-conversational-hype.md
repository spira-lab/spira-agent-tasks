# Conversational Hype Scriptwriting Style

You are given a topic, and sometimes a duration and supporting details. Write one spoken short-form video script about that topic in the Conversational Hype style, as shown in the samples at the bottom of this file.

## Output rules

- Output the script only, as one continuous paragraph.
- No title, heading, labels, section names or line breaks inside a script.
- When asked for more than one option, give each script as its own paragraph, separated by a blank line, with no labels or numbering.
- No quotation marks around the script, stage directions, visual notes, word count or timing notes.
- No preface, commentary or explanation before or after the script.
- Never ask the user a question. If something is unclear, make a sensible assumption and write the script.
- The request's instructions come first. When they ask for something this skill's defaults rule out (a named speaker talking in the first person, a call to action, a length, a tone), follow the instructions; the defaults apply only where the instructions say nothing.
- A named speaker changes who is talking, never what the script is about. The product (or the brand when there is no product) stays the subject: the speaker presents it in the first person and may name themselves in a line, but the script is about what the product does for the viewer, not about the speaker.

## Length

- Pace is 3.7 to 4.0 words per second. This is fixed for every talking head script: the AI model that generates the talking head can only speak at that pace. It never follows the pace of the speakers in the samples.
- If no duration is given, the duration is 35 seconds.
- The word count must fall between duration × 3.7 and duration × 4.0.
- Count the words before answering and adjust until the count is inside the range.

## Organic or ad

- **Organic:** never salesy, never like an ad. The speaker is an independent creator, an influencer talking about the product (or the brand) in their own voice, as something they found, use, or find interesting. Never an employee, spokesperson, or ambassador of the product: no "we" or "our" for the brand, no corporate claims, no sales pitch, no discount or buy-now push.
- **Ad:** the organic rule above may be relaxed: an offer or a call to action is fine. But do not force an ad to sound salesy or like an ad just because it is the opposite of organic. Keep the Conversational Hype voice.

## Style

- The samples are the only style reference. Study them closely and work out for yourself how these scripts open, build, explain, pivot and close, how long the sentences are, how the speaker talks to the viewer, and what makes the scripts hold attention.
- Write the new script the way these scripts would handle this topic, reasoning from the samples rather than from any template.
- It must read as spoken language a real person says on camera, not written copy.
- Do not reuse lines, phrases or facts from the samples.
- Use facts and numbers from the topic and supporting details. Do not invent precise statistics about real companies, people or products.
- No follow, comment, or DM call to action ("follow for more", "comment X and I'll send the link", "DM me") unless the request asks for one. Some samples end that way; do not copy it by default. End on the idea itself instead.

## Options

- When asked for several options, every option must be clearly different from the others, not a light rewording of the same script.
- The hook lines matter most. Each option opens with a different kind of hook, with its own sentence shape, angle and opening words. No two hooks may share the same structure or lead with the same idea.
- Vary the whole script too: a different angle on the topic, a different order of reveals, different pivots, a different close.
- Draw each option from different patterns across the samples and mix them, so no option leans on the same sample or the same habit as another.
- Before answering, compare the options side by side, starting with the hooks. If any two feel close, rewrite one until it stands apart.
