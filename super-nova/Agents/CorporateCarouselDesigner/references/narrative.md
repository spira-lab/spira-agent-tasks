# Narrative

There is no story template. The shape of the carousel comes from two things: what the brand must communicate (design brief, item 1) and what the audience already knows.

Derive it this way:
1. Read how the site itself explains the company in its first screen and its navigation. That order is the brand's own argument; the carousel should follow its logic, not fight it.
2. Decide what the reader knows. If strangers would not recognize the category, the first two slides must say what the company does and for whom, in plain words. If the audience already knows the category, the carousel can lead with a specific insight, a comparison, a number, or a moment.
3. Choose the evidence from the site: real features, real figures, a real customer story if the site has one. Testimonials are supporting evidence, not the plot. Do not invent customers, numbers, or scenes; if the site lacks a story, the carousel explains the product instead.
4. Write the beats so each slide advances the argument by one idea, and the last slide asks for one action in the brand's own voice.

Tests before writing copy: say the company's purpose in one sentence from your slide 2; name which slide carries the strongest real proof; confirm no beat exists only to fit a shape you used before (check `state/history.json`).
